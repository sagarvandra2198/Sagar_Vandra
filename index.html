Design Objective

The project focused on developing a dedicated mechanical fixture for the controlled formation of corners and seamless joining of inflatable gaskets.

The primary objective was to improve geometric accuracy, joint consistency and assembly repeatability while reducing manual handling errors and manufacturing waste.

Problem Identification & Fixture Concept

Manual positioning during gasket corner forming and joining can introduce variations in component orientation, corner geometry and joint alignment. These deviations can affect fit and the quality of the finished gasket.

The fixture concept established a defined locating and support arrangement to maintain the required geometry throughout processing.

The design aimed to constrain unwanted movement, improve alignment and provide a stable interface for forming and joining operations.

Transmission System Upgrade of a 4 Spindle Wire Drawing Machine

SHAUNAK INDUSTRIES · MECHANICAL DESIGN & TRANSMISSION

Transmission redesign & supporting mechanical modifications to increase wire drawing speed, improve speed coordination & enhance operational stability.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Engineering a Higher Speed Drawing Process

The project involved upgrading a 4 spindle wire drawing machine to increase its reported operating speed from 6 m/s to 12 m/s.

The existing belt driven transmission was affected by slippage, belt wear and rotational speed variations, which contributed to inconsistent drawing conditions and wire breakage.

The redesign focused on synchronous timing belt transmission, pulley ratio optimization and modifications to selected shafts, bearings and structural components.

INITIAL SPEED

6 m/s

REPORTED SPEED

12 m/s

REPORTED DRAWING SPEED · 100% INCREASE

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

From Friction Drive to Synchronous Transmission

EXISTING SYSTEM

V-Belt Transmission

Friction-based power transmission was susceptible to slip, creep, wear and speed variations, affecting coordination between spindle stages.

REDESIGN APPROACH

Synchronous Timing Belts

A toothed belt and pulley arrangement was introduced for more consistent shaft speed ratios, subject to correct engagement, tensioning and alignment.

The redesign required coordination of spindle speeds with the changing wire diameter and linear velocity at each drawing stage. Simply increasing every shaft speed by the same factor would not necessarily maintain stable wire tension.

TRANSMISSION UPGRADE

Implementing the Revised Drive Arrangement

The inter shaft V-belt transmission was replaced with synchronous timing belts and pulleys to establish the required rotational speed relationships.

The upgrade also involved modifications to selected shafts, bearing arrangements and structural components to accommodate the revised transmission configuration.

The mechanical arrangement was intended to support improved transmission repeatability, shaft positioning and operational stability at the increased drawing speed.

DESIGN PRIORITIES

  • — Synchronous speed coordination
  • — Shaft alignment and support
  • — Belt clearance and accessibility
  • — Reliable transmission operation

Shaft & Pulley Layout Development

Coordinating Shafts, Pulleys & Drive Ratios

The AutoCAD layout documents the motor input, individual spindle shafts and pulley arrangements within the transmission system.

ENGINEERING CALCULATIONS

Kinematics & Transmission Analysis

Fundamental relationships used to evaluate wire drawing velocity, cross-sectional reduction, spindle speed and synchronous belt transmission ratios.

01_VOLUME FLOW CONTINUITY

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂

A = πd² / 4

A = cross-sectional area (m²)
v = wire velocity (m/s)
d = wire diameter (m)

02_DRAWING VELOCITY

d₁²v₁ = d₂²v₂

v₂ = v₁ (d₁ / d₂)²

d₁, d₂ = initial and final wire diameters
v₁, v₂ = corresponding wire velocities

03_SPINDLE KINEMATICS

v = πDn / 60

v = linear wire speed (m/s)
D = effective contact diameter (m)
n = spindle speed (RPM)

04_SYNCHRONOUS BELT DRIVE

n₂ / n₁ = Z₁ / Z₂

n₂ = n₁ (Z₁ / Z₂)

n₁, n₂ = driving and driven shaft speeds
Z₁, Z₂ = driving and driven pulley tooth counts

Assumptions: steady-state drawing, approximately constant material volume flow and negligible transmission slip. The belt ratio equation assumes correct tooth engagement.

MECHANICAL INTEGRATION

Structural Mounting & Pivot Interface

A 12 mm thick steel plate was welded to the drum housing support structure to establish the primary mounting interface.

Two mounting blocks were welded to the support plate at the upper and lower positions specified in the design drawings. These blocks provided the attachment points for the motor mounting arrangement.

The motor was fastened to its mounting plate using nuts and bolts. A specially designed pivot pin, retained by a cotter pin, connected the motor plate to the supporting brackets.

This arrangement established the mechanical interface required for adjustment of the motor position through the belt tensioning mechanism.

SPACE CONSTRAINTS & LAYOUT

Under-Drum Housing Integration

The region beneath the drum housing defined the installation envelope for the additional motor and its associated drive components. The existing supporting geometry restricted the positioning of the mounting assembly.

The tapered support bracket and its belt clearance gap influenced the motor position, belt routing and mounting arrangement.

Layout development considered the relative positioning of the motor, pulleys, belt path, brackets and surrounding structure to identify potential geometric interference and maintain the required installation clearances.

DESIGN PRIORITIES

Mounting envelope · Belt clearance · Alignment · Accessibility

BELT TENSIONING MECHANISM

Threaded Adjustment & Controlled Repositioning

A dedicated tensioning plate with welded supporting brackets was incorporated into the mechanism. A threaded stud was installed between the brackets using a specially designed retaining pin.

The tensioning plate incorporated a rib structure to improve rigidity under the loads associated with belt adjustment. A slot in the motor mounting plate accommodated the threaded stud and allowed the required relative movement.

Adjustment nuts positioned on both sides of the motor mounting plate along the threaded stud enabled the plate to be repositioned relative to its support.

This movement changed the centre distance between the drive pulleys, providing a means of adjusting belt tension within the available installation envelope.

ENGINEERING OUTCOME

A Compact Integrated Mounting & Tensioning Solution

The developed assembly combined motor support and belt tension adjustment within a single compact arrangement tailored to the available space beneath the drum housing.

The design integrated a welded support plate, mounting blocks, pinned connections, a rib-reinforced tensioning plate and a threaded adjustment mechanism.

AutoCAD dimensional analysis and layout verification supported the assessment of component positioning, potential interference, belt passage and mounting clearances during design development.

The resulting concept was intended to provide appropriate belt alignment and tension adjustment while preserving clearance around the existing machine structure.

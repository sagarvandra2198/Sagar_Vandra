PROJECT 01 / OVERVIEW

From spatial constraint
to validated system.

The project focused on the mechanical design of a containerized experimental facility for district heating substations. The work combined spatial planning, 3D CAD, piping integration, component coordination and hydraulic assessment into a fabrication-oriented system-level design.

The initial 20-ft container configuration proved insufficient for the required equipment and service access. The final concept was therefore developed around a 40-ft ISO container accommodating seven HAST units together with the associated hydraulic and mechanical infrastructure.

PROJECT OBJECTIVE

Bringing photovoltaic operating data into one interface.

The objective was to develop a software-based monitoring solution capable of retrieving photovoltaic operating measurements remotely through the Shelly Cloud API. The acquired information was processed and displayed through a LabVIEW graphical interface, while time-series measurements were stored for subsequent analysis.

The system combined remote data acquisition, application-level processing, real-time visualization and automated logging, reducing the need for the operator to remain physically present at the photovoltaic installation.

Monitoring, selection and visualization.

WESTERN POLYRUB / MECHANICAL DESIGN

Assembly Fixture for Inflatable Seals

Design and development of a precision mechanical fixture for inflatable gasket corner forming and seam joining, with a focus on geometric consistency, repeatable positioning and manufacturing process improvement.

PROJECT FOCUS

Precision Fixture Design & Assembly Tooling

ENGINEERING OUTCOME

25% reported improvement in production efficiency

Design Objective

The project focused on developing a dedicated mechanical fixture for the controlled formation of corners and seamless joining of inflatable gaskets.

The primary objective was to improve geometric accuracy, joint consistency and assembly repeatability while reducing manual handling errors and manufacturing waste.

Problem Identification & Fixture Concept

Manual positioning during gasket corner forming and joining can introduce variations in component orientation, corner geometry and joint alignment. These deviations can affect fit and the quality of the finished gasket.

The fixture concept established a defined locating and support arrangement to maintain the required geometry throughout processing.

The design aimed to constrain unwanted movement, improve alignment and provide a stable interface for forming and joining operations.

CAD Modelling & Mechanical Design

A custom fixture was developed using CAD modelling to define its geometry, locating arrangement and assembly interfaces. The design translated functional manufacturing requirements into a practical tooling concept.

Geometric Positioning

Establishing repeatable gasket alignment during corner formation.

Mechanical Support

Providing stable support to limit unwanted movement during processing.

Joining Accessibility

Maintaining access to the relevant gasket regions during joining.

Dimensional Repeatability

Reducing variation in corner geometry and joint positioning between assemblies.

Manufacturability

Developing a practical fixture suitable for fabrication and integration into the existing production workflow.

Design & Fixture References

The project documentation includes the gasket cross-section design and reference images of fixtures for forming and joining.

Functional Role in Manufacturing

The fixture was intended to control gasket positioning during corner formation and joining, helping maintain the required geometry at transition regions.

Consistent positioning supported more uniform corner formation and alignment between relevant gasket sections. The locating arrangement reduced dependence on operator-controlled positioning and manual adjustment.

Airtight sealing remains dependent on the complete manufacturing process, including material condition, joining parameters and appropriate quality inspection.

Production Evaluation & Outcome

REPORTED PROJECT RESULT
25%

Improvement in production efficiency

Overall reported project improvement

Following the introduction of the custom fixture, the project reported a 25% improvement in production efficiency.

The reported improvements also included more uniform corner formation, reduced dimensional deviations, lower material waste and reduced assembly time.

The available project documentation does not specify the production baseline, measurement period or the separate contribution of individual process changes. The reported efficiency improvement therefore represents the overall project result.

Development of a dedicated mechanical tooling solution that translated gasket corner-forming and joining requirements into a practical fixture design, supporting repeatable positioning, consistent assembly quality and improved manufacturing efficiency.

01 / OVERVIEW

From physical reference to digital design.

The project involved reverse engineering an existing surgical instrument silicone mat and developing a CAD based design suitable for repeatable manufacturing.

The mat was intended to organise and safeguard sharp surgical instruments while supporting controlled placement, handling and storage.

The main engineering challenge was to reproduce the functional geometry of the reference component while improving dimensional consistency and manufacturability. The design needed to accommodate the intended instrument arrangement and provide suitable retention features.

02 / ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

03 / CAD & MANUFACTURING

Design beyond the model.

3D CAD modelling was used to define the mat geometry and its functional features. The model was developed with consideration for uniform moulding, repeatable component geometry and practical manufacturing requirements.

The design approach considered the relationship between feature dimensions, material behaviour and the ability to reproduce the required geometry consistently.
